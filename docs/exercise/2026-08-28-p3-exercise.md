# P3 Exercise Report, 2026-08-28

## What was exercised

The live P3 system: the running `aion-mcp` service, its Neo4j and SQLite stores, and the host Ollama serving `nomic-embed-text`, `qwen3:1.7b` and `qwen3:8b`. Not the test suite. Every claim below comes from a tool call, a graph read, a SQLite query, a service log line, or a direct probe of Ollama.

Window: the evening of 2026-08-28 local, roughly 22:30Z on 2026-08-28 through 01:10Z on 2026-08-29 by the service log. Four angles ran against one shared instance, concurrently and with overlapping windows, so each angle's numbers describe the instance under the load the others were also applying. One angle restarted the service at 23:47:55Z.

The four angles:

1. **Usage.** Realistic multi-session agent use: planning, debugging, refactoring, then next-day recall against what was stored. 22 recalls, 15 reflections, 12 episodes, 10 MCP sessions.
2. **Pipeline.** Targeted probes of the write path and the change-over-time machinery: designed correction batteries, contradiction bait, dedup probes, redaction probes. ~23 tracked episodes across 16 sessions, ~45 recalls.
3. **Robustness.** Adversarial and concurrency stress: secret payloads, malformed and extreme inputs, 8-way concurrent bursts, a mid-burst service restart, reconnect churn. ~1,700 reflection calls.
4. **Scale.** Load and growth behaviour: latency under contention, queue drain rate, hub degree, storage growth, activation termination, pack composition at size.

## Verdict

The write path earns its complexity; the read path does not yet. Single-episode extraction is the strongest thing in the system: all four angles judged the Decisions, Insights and Patterns faithful to their source prose, and none found a fabrication in them. The graph held its shape at forty times the episode count the defaults were tuned for, with seed p50 at 113ms, activation p50 at 64ms, and the token budget respected on 80 of 80 packs. Everything that reasons between nodes is currently net-negative. The relevance floor is inert on both search legs, so five queries on topics the substrate has never seen each returned twenty or more items and filled 1,161 to 1,199 of a 1,200-token budget with no degradation flag. Supersession fired three times in more than 100 enrichments and all three were wrong, auto-applied at confidence 1.0 against an empty proposal table, while a deliberate reversal produced zero edges; after four corrections were stored and fully enriched, three of four questions still answered with the pre-correction value, every item stamped current. Narratives are the sharpest failure: 27 words of source became eight sentences of invented history, they name none of a session's decisions on good input, and they are stored permanently with content vectors. None of this is architectural, since the graph, the bitemporal contract, per-session locking and crash recovery all held under concurrent multi-agent load including a live restart, but as exercised today this is a good recorder and an unreliable rememberer, and I would not let an agent act on a pack from it without checking the graph.

### Blockers

#### EX-1. The relevance floor is inert on both search legs, so recall cannot return an empty pack
**Area:** recall, quality. **Angles:** usage, pipeline, scale (independently, three times).

**Symptom.** A query with no relation to anything stored returns a full, budget-saturated pack of confident items with no degradation marker. An agent cannot tell a hit from a miss. The README's "an empty pack is a valid, honest answer" is unreachable.

**Repro.** Recall any topic absent from the substrate. Verified with 11 distinct queries across three angles, including "monsoon rainfall variability across Tamil Nadu districts", "how do I re-tension a bicycle wheel spoke", "quantum error correction surface codes for topological qubits", "zzqxwv plortnak vugglesnorf", "how many albatrosses did the Reykjavik ferry log in 1974".

**Evidence.** Every one returned a full pack. Sizes: 16 items/1,188 tokens, 20/1,157, 18/1,169, 13 facts + 6 episodes + 2 narratives/1,182, 20-27 items/1,161-1,199. The bicycle query returned `Feature flags are not re-read during the middle of a request. | bm25 1.00` at facts rank 2. The quantum query returned `ops surface (concept): ... maintenance burden of Kafka. | bm25 1.00` at rank 1; its cues were correct, so the failure is downstream of cue extraction. Across 1,284 logged recalls only 7 zero-item packs exist and all 7 have `seeds=0` from `as_of`/`knew_at` time-travel filters, never from topical irrelevance. Mean pack was 69.4 items pre-budget.

Two independent measurements of the mechanism. First, `normalizeToBest` divides every BM25 hit by the best hit for the same cue, so the top lexical hit scores exactly 1.00 for any query that matches any token. Second, the embedding noise floor was measured directly against host Ollama: 15 pairwise cosines over mutually unrelated sentences gave min 0.303, median 0.414, max 0.535; a separate probe gave unrelated pairs at 0.381, 0.412, 0.420, 0.434 against a genuinely related pair at 0.631. `AION_MIN_RELEVANCE` is 0.35, below the median similarity of unrelated text.

**Suspected cause.** Confirmed in code. `packages/core/src/recall/application/seeds.ts:123-136` (`normalizeToBest` returns `row.score / best`). `packages/core/src/infrastructure/config/defaults.ts:69` (`minRelevance: 0.35`). `packages/core/src/recall/domain/fusion.ts:282` computes `anchored` as `some(entry.relevance >= options.minRelevance)`, and line 287 sets `traversed = anchored && entry.activation !== undefined`, so one incidental lexical hit admits every activated node. The recency seed leg contributes seeds regardless of the query.

**Fix shape.** Gate on an absolute, per-method calibrated score: a corpus-calibrated raw BM25 threshold, and a cosine floor measured against this embedding model's noise floor (0.55 to 0.60 or higher for nomic-embed-text). Stop admitting traversed items that fail the floor on their own. Let the pack come back short or empty rather than filling to budget.

**Related.** EX-19 (what fills the slots), EX-21 (traversal truncation), EX-23 (vector-only weak matches), EX-30 (rendered pack misstates its own confidence), EX-38 (unbuilt legs).

#### EX-2. An AWS secret access key containing `/` is stored in plaintext
**Area:** substrate. **Angles:** usage, pipeline.

**Symptom.** A real-shaped AWS secret access key pasted in prose is written verbatim into Neo4j. The paired access-key id beside it is correctly redacted, so the payload looks handled. Nothing is ever hard-deleted, so the secret is permanent and recall-eligible. The reflection tool description states flatly: "Credentials are redacted before anything is stored."

**Repro.** `reflection` with an observation containing `wJalrXUtnFEMI/K7MDENG/bPxRfiCYEXAMPLEKEY` in prose, then read the stored Episode's `text`.

**Evidence.** Stored episode text, verbatim: `...pasted into the runbook: ⟨secret:aws-access-key:1a5d44⟩ with secret wJalrXUtnFEMI/K7MDENG/bPxRfiCYEXAMPLEKEY.` A controlled A/B in a single reflection call isolates the discriminator to one character: `Slash-free 40-char secret in prose: ⟨secret:high-entropy:00de14⟩ here.` versus `Slash-bearing 40-char secret in prose: wJalrXUtnFEMI/K7MDENG/bPxRfiCYEXAMPLEKEY here.` A direct call to the redaction module agrees: `redact('secret wJalrXUtnFEMI/K7MDENG/bPxRfiCYEXAMPLEKEY here')` returns `{matches: []}`, while the same string with `/` replaced by `x` returns `⟨secret:high-entropy:95754c⟩`.

**Suspected cause.** Confirmed in code. `packages/core/src/redaction/entropy.ts:7` is `const ENTROPY_TOKEN_PATTERN = /[A-Za-z0-9_+=-]{20,}/g`, which omits `/`, so the 40-char key splits into runs of 13, 7 and 18, none of which reaches the 20-char floor and none of which is ever entropy-scored. The file's own comment calls the cost "the rare secret that leans on them". AWS secrets are standard base64, so roughly half contain at least one `/`. The named rule at `packages/core/src/redaction/rules.ts:9-10` requires a literal `aws_secret_access_key` or `aws_secret_key` label adjacent to the value, which prose never supplies.

**Fix shape.** Add `/` (and the rest of the base64 alphabet) to the entropy token class, and suppress path and URL false positives with a context check on a preceding scheme or path separator rather than by removing the character class. Score the whole run rather than the split fragments.

**Note.** The usage angle reported the split as 13/9/17. That is arithmetically impossible for a 40-char string with two separators; the correct split is 13/7/18, confirmed by direct measurement. The finding itself is unaffected.

**Related.** EX-3 and EX-9 are two further, independent leak paths through the same module.

#### EX-3. Secrets arriving as JSON key/value pairs bypass every context-dependent rule
**Area:** substrate. **Angle:** robustness.

**Symptom.** Context-dependent secret rules need the key name and the value in the same string. When the deep-walk descends a `tool_execution` payload, an object key and its value are redacted as two independent strings, so the key-name context never reaches the value. Structured tool telemetry is the normal shape of agent traces, so this is the common case, not an edge case.

**Repro.** `reflection` with `tool_executions:[{tool:'exec_shell', input:{command:'env', context:{nested:{aws_secret_access_key:'wJalrXUtnFEMI/K7MDENG/bPxRfiCYEXAMPLEKEY'}}}}]`. The same secret written inline as prose in a turn of the same call was correctly redacted, which proves the gap is JSON-shape-specific rather than a missing rule.

**Evidence.** Live graph read of episode `d4492ee7`, node property `text` contains verbatim: `"input":{"command":"env","context":{"nested":{"aws_secret_access_key":"wJalrXUtnFEMI/K7MDENG/bPxRfiCYEXAMPLEKEY"}}}`. A parameterized `CONTAINS` sweep across every string property of every node and relationship confirmed the hit persisted after full P3 enrichment completed.

**Suspected cause.** Confirmed in code. `packages/core/src/redaction/deep-walk.ts:45-46`: `for (const [key, entry] of Object.entries(value)) { out[redactString(key, ...)] = walk(entry, ...) }`. The key goes through `redactString` alone and the value goes through `walk` alone. `rules.ts:9-10` (`AWS_SECRET_KEY`) and `rules.ts:52-53` (`GENERIC_SECRET_ASSIGNMENT`) both require `name<delimiter>value` in one string.

**Fix shape.** For object entries, run context-dependent rules against a synthesized `key: value` string, or pass the sibling key to `redactString` as context for the value, before falling back to the entropy backstop.

**Related.** EX-2, EX-9.

#### EX-4. A partial-failure retry re-mints cognitive nodes, permanently and without bound
**Area:** worker, reflection-pipeline. **Angles:** usage, pipeline, robustness (independently, three times).

**Symptom.** A single stage timeout discards the whole enrichment run but not its graph writes. The job retries up to 5 times. The cognitive stage has no ledger gate and its only idempotency mechanism is a content hash over LLM-generated text, which does not collide because the model paraphrases differently on each pass. Every retry MERGEs a new set of near-duplicate cognitive nodes. The result is permanent, since nothing is hard-deleted, and often mis-typed.

**Repro.** Let an episode's `semantic-relationships` stage exceed its 60s abort under Ollama contention, which happened organically on a first real payload with no contrived trigger. Watch the retry and count `EXTRACTED_FROM` nodes after each pass.

**Evidence.** Episode `d4492ee7`, run 1 at 115.8s: 8 stages ok, `semantic-relationships` failed with `semantic relationship call timed out: AbortError`, `applied:false`, and counts `{entities:25, associations:305, cognitive:16, contextVectors:48}` already written. The log line reads `reflection produced nothing; ledger left open`. Run 2 at 101.7s: `applied:true`, `{entities:0, cognitive:9, ...}`. Entities were idempotent; cognitive nodes were not. That episode now holds 25 cognitive nodes in paired near-duplicates, including two Goals ("Rotate all exposed credentials to secure the system." and "Rotate all exposed credentials to mitigate security risks.") and two entirely new node types that did not exist after pass 1, so the model's own category assignment is unstable between runs.

Episode `2df277b3` went further. Interrupted by a SIGTERM, reclaimed after the stale-claim timeout, then failed on `semantic-relationships` three more times before `reflection job exhausted its attempts; left queued for maintenance`. Counted at 44 cognitive nodes including 10 Decisions by one angle, and at 63 (all distinct text) by a later count from another angle. Its byte-identical twin `836e2da6` has 12, a ratio of 5.25x. Several of the extras are mis-typed: `Decision: PostgreSQL took the full read load, overwhelming the database.` and `Decision: Redis evicted session keys under memory pressure` are observations, corrupting the one node type decision queries depend on. Cost: three failed attempts consumed 115.7s + 109.6s + 155.2s = 380.5s, which is 63% of that window, against 185.5s for the three real enrichments in the same period, with 4,000+ jobs queued behind. One discarded run also wrote `supersessions: 1`, an irreversible validity closure applied during a run the orchestrator then declared "produced nothing".

**Suspected cause.** Confirmed in code. `packages/core/src/reflection/domain/stage.ts:100-102`: `shouldMarkApplied` returns `stages.length > 0 && stages.every((stage) => stage.status !== 'failed')`. Each stage writes eagerly and irreversibly. `packages/core/src/reflection/application/worker.ts:319` turns that into `this.#fail(job, 'no stage enriched ' + episodeId)` and a full-pipeline retry. `grep -l LedgerApplied` over `stages/` returns `reinforcement.ts`, `associations.ts`, `entity-dedup.ts` only; `cognitive.ts` has zero ledger references and relies on `deriveCognitiveNodeId` (episode + type + normalized text hash), whose own docstring calls that "the whole idempotency mechanism".

**Fix shape.** Make the ledger per-stage so a replay runs only the stages that have not applied, gate the cognitive stage with its own episode-scoped ledger key the way `associations` and `reinforcement` are gated, treat an all-stages-already-applied replay as success, and make the failure message name the stage that failed. The current message is also false: it claims nothing was enriched when 8 of 9 stages did.

**Related.** EX-34 (exhausted jobs never leave the queue), EX-10 (the worker time this burns).

#### EX-5. Correcting a stored fact does not change what recall answers
**Area:** quality. **Angle:** pipeline.

**Symptom.** After four corrections were stored and fully enriched, three of four questions still returned the pre-correction value at the top of the pack, every item labelled `currency: current`.

**Repro.** Store baselines (Stripe webhook retry limit is 3 attempts; billing service deploys to AWS us-east-1; Ryan prefers squash merges). Wait for enrichment. Store the corrections (7 attempts; Fly.io; merge commits). Wait for enrichment. Re-ask.

**Evidence.** "what is the stripe webhook retry limit" returned ranks 1 through 4 all saying 3 attempts, all current: `webhook (concept): The Stripe webhook retry limit is 3 attempts.`, `retry limit (concept): ...`, `Stripe (organization): ...`, and the source Concept. The number 7 appears nowhere in the pack. "where is the billing service deployed" returned ranks 2 through 4 as AWS us-east-1, current, with Fly.io absent from the top 5. "what does Ryan prefer for merging pull requests" returned ranks 1 through 3 as squash merges, and the Decision `Ryan decided to prefer merge commits over squash merges to maintain bisectability` was absent from the top 5. Only the AWS case hedged, at rank 1: `us-east-1 (location): The AWS region where the billing service no longer deploys`.

**Suspected cause.** Three compounding causes, each its own finding: entity text is written once and never revised (EX-18), the correction and the baseline land under different cognitive labels so supersession never scans them (EX-6), and fusion has no recency preference between competing factual claims.

**Fix shape.** Resolve competing claims about the same subject by recorded time in fusion, and re-derive an entity's served description from its current facts rather than freezing the first episode's phrasing.

**Related.** EX-6, EX-7, EX-17, EX-18.

#### EX-6. Supersession misses real corrections: zero true positives across two designed batteries
**Area:** reflection-pipeline. **Angles:** usage, pipeline.

**Symptom.** Sessions that explicitly reverse an earlier decision produce zero SUPERSEDES edges and zero proposals. Old and new decisions coexist as `current` and recall serves them side by side with no lineage marker.

**Repro.** Two independent batteries. First: reflect "outbox table plus a poller, not Kafka", then 7 minutes later reflect "So we went back on the outbox poller. We are doing Pub/Sub push to a Cloud Run endpoint now, because the burst on close day is twenty times our steady state." Second: store six baseline operational facts, wait for enrichment, store six corrections (four genuine, two false-bait), wait, then read `supersessions` and `supersession_proposals`.

**Evidence.** Reversal case: the enrichment log for the reversal episode reads `"supersessions":0,"supersessionProposals":0`. Both Decisions are live with `valid_until = null`. Their cosine is 0.569, below `DEFAULT_CONTRADICTION_NEIGHBOR_THRESHOLD = 0.75`, so the judge was never handed the pair. Phrasing sensitivity is severe: the old decision measured against three natural statements of the new one gave 0.804, 0.856 and 0.670, so a concise restatement is silently skipped. The extractor also inverted the new decision, minting `Revert to the outbox poller and implement Pub/Sub push to address the burst volume issue.`, which says to use the thing being abandoned. Recall on "do we still use the outbox poller, or did we replace it?" returned the correct fact, the stale fact, and the inverted Decision, all marked `current`, with no `superseded_by` marker anywhere in the pack.

Six-case battery: 0 true positives, 0 proposals, 4 misses, 1 cross-subject false positive, 3 false-baits passing vacuously because the stage was skipped. Every baseline landed as Concept or Event, not Decision or Insight: `Stripe webhook retry limit is set to 3 attempts...` (Concept), `Twilio SMS callback has a timeout of 30 seconds.` (Concept), `Nightly reconciliation job takes 4 hours to complete` (Event), `Deployment of billing service to AWS us-east-1` (Event). The one case that did mint a Decision (`Ryan decided to prefer merge commits over squash merges`) returned `supersessions=0, proposals=0`.

**Suspected cause.** Confirmed in code. `packages/core/src/infrastructure/graph/supersession-queries.ts:23`: `FACT_NODE_LABELS = ['Decision', 'Insight']`. Substrate-wide the eligible labels are a minority: Concept 100, Event 73, Insight 36, Decision 32, Goal 32, Context 32, Plan 21, Pattern 16, Trend 8. Candidates come only from embedding proximity at `DEFAULT_CONTRADICTION_NEIGHBOR_THRESHOLD = 0.75` (`stages/supersession.ts:43`) against the same label, with no subject identity check, and subjects are the first 6 nodes ordered alphabetically by `text_norm` (`supersession-queries.ts:55`, `ORDER BY n.text_norm, n.id`).

**Fix shape.** Seed contradiction candidates from the episode's own entities rather than from embedding proximity between claim texts, since both sides of a real reversal name the same subject. Include Concept and Event in the scan, or route any statement carrying a named parameter and a value into a label the stage actually reads. Pick subjects by salience rather than alphabetical truncation.

**Related.** EX-5 (the user-visible outcome), EX-7 (the precision half), EX-17.

#### EX-7. When the supersession judge does fire it is wrong, and it auto-applies irreversibly
**Area:** reflection-pipeline. **Angles:** pipeline (blocker), usage (major). Higher severity taken.

**Symptom.** Across more than 100 enrichments the judge fired 3 times. All 3 are false positives, auto-applied at confidence 1.0, closing a node's validity interval with no human in the loop. `supersession_proposals` has never held a row.

**Repro.** `MATCH (new)-[:SUPERSEDES]->(old) WHERE old:Decision OR old:Insight RETURN old.text, new.text, old.valid_until`, then `select count(*) from supersession_proposals`.

**Evidence.** All three firings, quoted from the graph:

1. OLD `The retry backoff was capped at four attempts to prevent duplicate captures.` (from an unrelated sibling episode about the payments worker) superseded by NEW `Raise the retry limit from 3 to 7 attempts...` (a Stripe webhook change). Two different services' retry policies, both still true.
2. OLD `Sarah Chen believes Meridian and Halyard can run in parallel, reducing the need for migration pause.` superseded by NEW `Marcus Delgado wants the Meridian migration paused until Halyard is on the new PostgreSQL primary`. The source episode says explicitly that Sarah Chen disagrees. The substrate has now recorded that one engineer's stated position is no longer current because a colleague disagreed.
3. OLD `The unbounded response cache was the direct cause of the OOM crash loop, not the Redis eviction that caused the checkout outage.` superseded by NEW `The OOM crash loop was a separate issue from the checkout outage, which was caused by Redis eviction.` The same claim restated; the vaguer version closed the more precise one.

All carry `confidence: 1`, provenance `['reflection_supersession']`. `select count(*) from supersession_proposals` returns 0 for the lifetime of the install. Firing 2 was written during a run the orchestrator subsequently discarded as "produced nothing" (EX-4). Two of the three trace to cross-session duplicate episode pairs (EX-29).

**Suspected cause.** `packages/core/src/reflection/application/stages/supersession.ts:36`, `DEFAULT_SUPERSEDE_AUTO_CONFIDENCE = 0.85`. The model emits 1.0 on near-neighbours that share vocabulary, so 0.85 is below every confidence it actually produces and the proposal path is dead code. The judge draws no distinction between "contradicts", "restates", and "disagrees with".

**Fix shape.** Require an explicit shared-subject check (the same entity or service named on both sides) before a judgment counts. Drop auto-apply for a local single-user substrate, or raise the bar above what the model actually emits; write proposals and surface them in `aion doctor` or a review command. Teach the judge to separate correction from restatement and from unresolved disagreement.

**Related.** EX-6, EX-14 (SUPERSEDES is overloaded, so lineage is unreadable), EX-29.

#### EX-8. Session narratives are confabulated and stored as permanent, recall-eligible memory
**Area:** reflection-pipeline, quality. **Angles:** usage (blocker), pipeline (major). Higher severity taken.

**Symptom.** Narratives are first-class Memory nodes with content vectors, recall-eligible forever. On thin input they are outright fabrications. On realistic input they are content-free platitudes that name none of the session's actual facts. Either way they read like recorded history.

**Repro.** Store a session, let it close or idle, then diff the Narrative node against the episode text.

**Evidence.** Fabrication. Source episode, verbatim and complete: `summary: close-mode probe terminate / observation: Close-hook probe using terminate. One observation so the session has an episode to narrate.` Stored narrative: "The session began with the decision to terminate a close-mode probe, likely due to system constraints or mission changes. The probe, operating in close-mode, was presumably gathering detailed data from a target. The termination command was issued without further explanation... No additional data was collected after the termination." 27 words of source became eight sentences of invented surveillance fiction.

Invention on realistic input. A credential-rotation episode of 3 lines, one of which recorded that a live key was pasted into a runbook, produced "The process involved generating new keys and updating the configuration files accordingly. No disruptions were reported during the transition. The user verified the new credentials were functioning properly... All relevant stakeholders were informed of the update." None of that is in the source, and the one operationally important fact was dropped.

Wrong architecture invented. An episode stating four decisions (do not shard the orders table; move session state to signed cookies; ship Meridian behind a flag with two-week shadow reads; do not write to the finops-owned billing table) produced: "Decisions were made to adopt a microservices architecture to improve system modularity. A service mesh was selected to manage inter-service communication securely and efficiently." Not one of those appears in the episode. The OOM episode's narrative invents mitigations ("increasing memory limits or implementing periodic garbage collection") that were never proposed; the episode says bound the cache to 500 entries.

Platitude on good input. A 3-episode planning session's narrative names none of its four decisions (no Postgres outbox, no Kafka, no sha256, no GCS, no Datastream CDC). It says "four decisions being finalized and two alternative approaches being rejected", then "The rejected alternatives were evaluated for potential future use" (invented) and "The session demonstrated a structured approach to system design" (platitude). A debugging session's narrative says "the agent identified the root cause of the duplicates and implemented a fix" without naming either.

Shape. Every narrative read was 8 or 9 sentences ending in "The session concluded/ended with...". 12 of 12 sampled summaries begin "The session focused on..." or "The session involved...". A 41-char episode produced a 493-char narrative, a 12x expansion; every 1-episode narrative expands its source between 2.3x and 12x, and 1-source narratives average 820 chars against 701 for 7-source ones, so length is a target rather than a function of content. These reach packs: narratives appeared in 5 of 22 recalls in one angle's set and were the correct session's narrative once.

**Suspected cause.** `packages/core/src/reflection/application/narratives.ts`, `DEFAULT_NARRATIVE_MAX_TOKENS = 700` with a free-form prose prompt on `qwen3:8b`. A length target with no grounding constraint forces padding, and padding on thin input becomes invention.

**Fix shape.** Constrain the narrative to extracted nodes rather than free prose: a structured output listing decisions, insights and events by reference, with a hard rule to state nothing not present in the source. Bound length as a fraction of source length. Let it be two sentences when the session was two sentences.

**Related.** EX-24 (the same nodes become degree-580 hubs), EX-16 (the other generative stage with the same discipline problem).

### Major

#### EX-9. The entropy backstop swings on the casing of an adjacent key name
**Area:** substrate. **Angle:** robustness. Reported as blocker; downgraded to major after verification, see the note.

**Symptom.** The same secret is redacted or leaked depending on the case of the env-var-style key name beside it, because Shannon entropy is computed over the whole greedy matched token (key name plus value) rather than over the candidate secret span. The 4.5 bits/char threshold sits inside the gap that creates.

**Repro.** Send the same value twice in one reflection call: once as `aws_access_key_id=<value>` in prose, once as `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID=<value>\n` inside a `tool_execution.output.stdout` string.

**Evidence.** Live graph: the turn text shows `aws_access_key_id=⟨secret:high-entropy:b1a49f⟩` while the tool output shows `"stdout":"AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID=AKIAABCDEFGHIJ23456\nGITHUB_TOKEN=⟨secret:github-token:4da9ce⟩\n"`. Measured directly: `shannonEntropy('aws_access_key_id=AKIAABCDEFGHIJ23456')` = 4.608, above the 4.5 threshold; `shannonEntropy('AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID=AKIAABCDEFGHIJ23456')` = 4.145, below it. Uppercase is the standard shell and AWS CLI convention, so an `env` dump piped into a tool execution is the leaking shape.

**Suspected cause.** Confirmed in code. `entropy.ts:7` includes `_` and `=` in the token class, so `KEY_NAME=value` matches as one token, and `entropy.ts:59` scores `match[0]`, the whole token.

**Note on severity.** The specific value that leaked is `AKIA` plus 15 characters. `rules.ts:6` is `/\bAKIA[0-9A-Z]{16}\b/`, which needs 16, so the named rule could not fire on this synthetic value and it fell through to the entropy backstop. A well-formed access key id is caught by the named rule, and the usage angle's `AKIAIOSFODNN7EXAMPLE` (AKIA plus 16) was in fact redacted as `⟨secret:aws-access-key:1a5d44⟩`. The headline "AWS access key ID leaks" therefore overstates the case for real AWS ids. The general defect stands and is unaffected: the entropy backstop is the only protection for every secret with no named rule, and its verdict is decided by the casing of an adjacent word.

**Fix shape.** Score entropy over sliding sub-windows, or over the longest run of base64/hex-alphabet characters after splitting on case and underscore boundaries, so a key name cannot swing a real secret across the threshold.

**Related.** EX-2, EX-3.

#### EX-10. Enrichment throughput, intake backpressure, and operational visibility
**Area:** worker. **Angles:** all four.

**Symptom.** A single serial worker retires roughly 1.9 to 6.7 episodes per minute while intake accepts about 8 per second and always returns `queued: true`. There is no backpressure, no queue cap, no per-session fairness, and no queue metric anywhere a caller or operator would see it. `aion doctor` reports all green while the substrate is hours behind.

#### EX-25. `reinforcement_queue` grows without bound, has no consumer, and voids edge weighting
**Area:** substrate. **Angles:** scale, pipeline, usage.

**Symptom.** Every recall writes 45 rows to a table nothing reads. It is now the large majority of the SQLite file. Because the flush that would consume it does not exist, every CO_OCCURS and SIMILAR edge keeps a constant strength, which silently voids activation's evidence-scaled weighting.

**Repro.** `select trigger, count(*) from reinforcement_queue group by 1`, then `select name, sum(pgsize) from dbstat group by name order by 2 desc`, then grep for any read of the table.

**Evidence.** Row count over the exercise: 68,519, then 68,745, then 70,101 to 79,593 (+9,492 in ~90 minutes). Composition at the end: `recall_co_activation` 65,610 and `reflection:co-extraction` 10,154, over 8,089 distinct pairs. Rate: 44.7 rows per recall served (65,610 / 1,469), matching C(10,2) = 45 from `REINFORCEMENT_TOP_N = 10` (`side-effects.ts:25`), plus 270 rows/min under load. `dbstat`: the table is 13,340,672 bytes plus a 3,792,896-byte autoindex, which is 17.1 MB of an 18.9 MB file (89%); the next largest object is `last_pack` at 1.09 MB. Projection at 200 recalls/day: 9,000 rows/day, roughly 270k rows and 61 MB/month, unbounded.

Consequence in the graph: all 1,550 CO_OCCURS edges have min = max = avg strength 1.0 and confidence 1.0, and 274 of 288 SIMILAR edges are at confidence 1.0. `activation.ts:66` documents SIMILAR and RELATED_TO as "ceilings rather than fixed weights ... scaled by strength x confidence", which with constant 1.0 makes them fixed weights after all.

**Suspected cause.** Confirmed in code. `packages/core/src/infrastructure/sqlite/reinforcement-queue.ts` contains exactly one INSERT (line 40) and one unfiltered `SELECT * FROM reinforcement_queue ORDER BY rowid ASC` (line 48). No DELETE, no drain, no TTL. `packages/core/src/recall/application/side-effects.ts:44` enqueues inline on every recall. The P4 plasticity flush that would consume the rows is not built.

**Fix shape.** Cap the table by age or row count now, since a rolling window is closer to Hebbian semantics than an unbounded log anyway. Dedupe on `(source, target)` so repeats increment a count rather than appending a row. Surface its size in `doctor`.

#### EX-43. What held up
**Area:** all. **Angles:** robustness, pipeline, usage, scale.

Recorded because it bounds the fix scope: the failures above are in ranking, prompt discipline and operational visibility, not in the structure.

**Concurrency and crash safety.** Ten concurrent identical reflection calls in one session produced exactly 1 episode and exactly 1 queue row. Three sessions of three interleaved identical calls produced exactly 3 episodes. An 8-way, 2-minute burst ran 1,159 stores and 1,159 recalls with zero errors of either kind. The global Session FOLLOWS chain audit across 154 sessions built under heavy concurrent multi-agent load found 153 with exactly one outgoing edge, 153 distinct priors, zero forks, zero cycles and exactly one chain root. A mid-burst service restart produced clean client error shapes at every stage (`aion-mcp is shutting down; retry once it is back`, then `fetch failed`, then a well-formed `{"code":-32600,"message":"unknown session: <id>"}`), with no data corruption and no cross-session contamination.

**Schema and input handling.** Empty-string recall query rejected; `budget.max_tokens=0` rejected; empty reflection payload rejected with a specific message ("reflection requires at least one of: turns, tool_executions, observations"); `as_of` predating the substrate correctly returns "No memories matched"; unicode, emoji, CJK, RTL and ZWJ content stored and rendered back byte-correct; a 500-level-deep nested tool_execution object stored in 599ms; a 200-turn, ~86KB session stored intact with `turn_count=200` and text length matching exactly.

**Extraction quality.** The content of single-episode extraction is the best part of the system. Faithful nodes with correct rationales and statuses, no hallucination found: `Decision: Drop received_at from the hash input because it is envelope metadata, not part of the adjudication identity.`; `Insight: An idempotency key must hash the identity of the event, not the envelope around it.`; `Pattern: Barrel exports can hide circular dependencies until re-exports are removed.`; `Decision: We will not shard the orders table.` with rationale "The read amplification is in the join, not the row count, and sharding moves the pain into the application layer."; `Decision: We reject the proposal to write to the finops-owned billing table directly.` with rationale "It is another team's data and we do not design writes into it."; `Goal: Get the Halyard ledger onto the new PostgreSQL primary.` status completed, and a Plan correctly marked abandoned. On-topic recall found the right node at rank 1 for "did we decide to shard the orders table", "what is our q3 latency goal" and "what did we decide about the finops billing table". Two blemishes inside the good half: Goal priority is fabricated when the episode states none ("priority: high" invented twice), and an unresolved disagreement was minted as two competing Decision nodes.

**Graph performance at 40x the tuned size.** Seeds went from p50 74ms to p50 113ms, activation from p50 25ms to p50 64ms. Vector and fulltext index probes stayed at 4 to 5ms. Hub adjacency at degree 586 read in 4 to 11ms. Hub inhibition kept the Member and the degree-581 narratives from flooding a pack. The token budget held on 80 of 80 packs, maximum 1,199 of 1,200.

**Honest degradation signals.** The `degraded` field reported every cue timeout correctly, `coverage` and `coverage_count` record the narrative gap honestly even though the edge write ignores it, and `aion last` gives a real trust surface. The cue cache is a large measured win (p50 1,484ms to 0.04ms).

## Live incident: queue self-starvation and operator triage

Mid-exercise, the system demonstrated the interactive-starved-behind-bulk failure mode live, unplanned, and better than any designed test. The robustness angle's concurrency burst (an exercise-harness spec with no rate cap: 8 sessions in a tight store loop, plus 4 restart-test sessions) pushed ~4,100 synthetic episodes in minutes. Intake accepted every one at ~8/second; the single enrichment worker retires 1.9 to 6.7 episodes/minute; the queue reached 4,016 unclaimed jobs, days of GPU work. Every legitimate probe episode from the other angles queued behind the flood in FIFO order. The angles' wait loops, expecting enrichment in minutes, would have resolved in days. An independent concurrent client hit the same wall from the outside: its three interactive episodes sat unclaimed 15 minutes after intake.

Triage required raw SQL inside the container (EX-41): purge the 3,986 unclaimed flood jobs, keep the 27 real ones. The queue drained within the hour and the exercise completed. Three product facts fell out: intake has no backpressure or admission control (EX-10); the queue has no priority lanes, so bulk starves interactive (the owner pinned the requirement the same day: interactive episodes enrich in minutes, bulk queues behind interactive, never the reverse); and the operator surface for all of this is nonexistent — no depth, no age, no lag gauge, no queue commands (EX-10, EX-41). One harness fact fell out too: exercise specs that say "tight loop" without a cap are load tests whether they mean to be or not.

## External corroboration: independent concurrent client

A second client session (code-e9) exercised the live stack concurrently and independently, from the raw streamable-HTTP boundary with a real semantic workload, and shared its findings for this merge. Where the two exercises overlap, they agree.

Corroborated findings: the enrichment backlog and freshness gap (its three episodes unclaimed 15 minutes post-intake; supports EX-10/EX-11 and the freshness pin); cue latency under co-located inference load (a 7.2s cue call against the 8000ms budget while reflection competed for Ollama; supports EX-12); entity-card staleness and attribute conflation (a canonical entity card asserting a superseded port value as current, with no per-attribute provenance; supports EX-17/EX-18); the soft-supersession miss (a decision reversal leaving both "leading candidate" and "final title" standing as current; supports EX-5/EX-6); and the inert relevance floor (a Lorem-ipsum fixture episode admitted to a grounding query; supports EX-1). One divergence resolved in this report's favor: its redaction PASS was measured at pack level only, and the stored-node reads behind EX-2/EX-3 supersede it (its findings file now records this).

Independently verified working from the outside: the protocol surface (session handshake, error shapes, `-32602` with exact field paths, clean unknown-session responses), item-level `knew_at` correctness, cross-session recall with currency labels on every item, redaction of well-formed credential shapes end to end from the wire, and the restart-reconnect story (a clean unknown-session error, immediate re-handshake). Its process-separation analysis also stands: the whitepaper's three-service split would not have prevented the backlog or the cue contention — both are shared-GPU inference capacity problems, and the Node event loop never blocked.

## Fix-planning input

Six candidate workstreams. Scope lines only; sequencing, sizing and ownership are the planning step's job, not this document's.

Three constraints from the owner frame the round (pinned 2026-08-28, after this exercise's evidence). First, freshness is a requirement, not a preference: asynchronous enrichment buys an unblocked hot path, never staleness — an interactive episode enriches in minutes, and the supersession lag window is a correctness window. Second, the fix ladder is quality-neutral-first: priority lanes and concurrent-request parallelism (`worker_count` plus `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL`) before anything that touches prompts; prompt batching only where the quality harness clears it per model (expected safe under Haiku routing, expected lossy on qwen3:8b). Third, shipping posture: local-default with sensible optimizations and a documented local-Ollama performance caveat, with Anthropic routing recommended for faster, better results. WS-E below is the direct implementation surface for the first two; WS-D's generative-stage work should route through the harness gate.

**WS-A. Retrieval floor and pack honesty.** Replace rank-normalized anchoring with absolute, per-method calibrated floors so a miss returns a thin or empty pack, then make the pack state its own rank, confidence and truncation. Covers EX-1, EX-19, EX-20, EX-21, EX-22, EX-23, EX-30, EX-38.

**WS-B. Write-path integrity: redaction and retry idempotency.** Close the three redaction leak paths (base64 alphabet, structured JSON key/value, greedy-token entropy scoring) and make the reflection ledger per-stage so a replay cannot re-mint cognitive nodes. Covers EX-2, EX-3, EX-4, EX-9, EX-34.

**WS-C. Change over time: supersession, corrections and entity identity.** Make a stored correction actually change the answer: subject-identity candidate generation, a label scope that includes where parameters land, propagation to derived nodes, no auto-apply without measured precision, and entity identity that can be merged safely and unmerged when wrong. Covers EX-5, EX-6, EX-7, EX-13, EX-14, EX-15, EX-17, EX-18, EX-29.

**WS-D. Generated-text discipline.** Constrain every generative stage to what its source supports: narratives grounded in extracted nodes and bounded by source length, typed relationships restricted to claim-bearing endpoints with a quoted justification, and a cognitive taxonomy that may return nothing for a type. Covers EX-8, EX-16, EX-24, EX-27, EX-36.

**WS-E. Throughput, contention and operational visibility.** Give the interactive recall path priority at the provider, surface queue depth, oldest-pending age, exhausted jobs and enrichment lag through `status`, `doctor`, `/health` and the reflection ack, and tell the caller when its own recent memory is not yet enriched. Covers EX-10, EX-11, EX-12, EX-33, EX-34, EX-40, EX-41.

**WS-F. Storage growth.** Bound the two unbounded stores: cap or TTL `reinforcement_queue` until its consumer ships, and set a Neo4j transaction-log retention policy. Covers EX-25, EX-26.

## Exclusions

Findings from the raw sets that are not carried above, or that are carried in a corrected form, with the reason.

1. **"An AWS access key ID leaks in tool output"** (robustness), carried as EX-9 with the headline corrected. The leaked value is `AKIA` plus 15 characters, and `rules.ts:6` requires `AKIA[0-9A-Z]{16}`, so the named rule could not fire on that synthetic value. A well-formed id is caught, proven by the usage angle's `AKIAIOSFODNN7EXAMPLE` being redacted as `⟨secret:aws-access-key:1a5d44⟩`. The underlying entropy-scoring defect is real and is kept; the AWS-specific framing is not.

2. **"The entropy tokens split into runs of 13/9/17"** (usage), replaced by 13/7/18. The stated split does not sum to a 40-character string with two separators; direct measurement of `wJalrXUtnFEMI/K7MDENG/bPxRfiCYEXAMPLEKEY` gives 13, 7, 18. The finding (EX-2) is unaffected.

3. **"Roughly 3,900 episodes were silently dropped from the enqueue path"** (scale), demoted to EX-41 as an observation about missing reconciliation rather than a defect. The angle states it could not reproduce the loss, its own 41 writes enqueued 41 for 41, and the affected episodes predate its session. Only the verifiable half is kept: no reconciliation exists and `doctor` passes in that state.

4. **"Supersession has never once produced a correct result"** (pipeline), narrowed. The claim is correct for the cognitive supersession stage (0 of 3) and is stated that way in EX-7. It is not correct for SUPERSEDES as a whole, since the same angle's own census records 1 legitimate Episode-to-Episode correction.

5. **"Server session objects accumulate"** (usage), qualified in EX-32. Accumulation is bounded by `#evictOverflow` at `service.ts:326-334`, which drops the oldest session past `MAX_SESSIONS`. There is no idle timer, so the finding and its fix shape stand, but the growth is capped rather than unbounded.

6. **Single-angle drain-rate figures presented as the drain rate.** Robustness measured 6.7 episodes/min and scale measured 1.89/min. Neither is excluded; both are reported in EX-10 with their conditions, and the extrapolation is given as a range rather than one number, because the two windows differed in concurrent load.

7. **Duplicate cognitive-node counts for episode `2df277b3`** (44 from usage, 63 from pipeline). Neither is wrong; the counts were taken at different times while the retries continued. EX-4 reports both with that framing rather than picking one.
